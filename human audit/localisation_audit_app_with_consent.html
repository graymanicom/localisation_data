Audit rules

Research context. This project studies how localisation is represented inside African-adapted language models. In this audit, localisation means whether a sentence makes sense in the South African institutional and social context.

Dataset aim. We are building sentence pairs for a classifier. The original sentence should be valid. The swapped sentence should be grammatically plausible but contextually wrong because the institution or document no longer fits.

Your role. You help filter out noisy signals. We do not want a classifier that only detects broken grammar or strange fragments. We want examples where the invalidity depends on local knowledge. Please do not share personal information.

  1. Judge whether the original sentence is grammatically and semantically valid.
  2. Judge whether the rule-swapped sentence is grammatical.
  3. Judge whether the rule-swapped sentence is invalid in its South African context.
  4. If the rule-swapped version fails either test, judge the LLM-repaired version too.
  5. Choose the version that should be kept: rule, LLM, or neither.
Example

Original: Apply for an identity document at Home Affairs.

Swapped: Apply for an identity document at SASSA.

This may be grammatically correct, but invalid in the South African context because SASSA is not the correct institution for identity documents.

Important distinction. “Semantically invalid” here does not mean impossible in every imaginable world. It means the sentence no longer makes sense in ordinary South African public-service context.
